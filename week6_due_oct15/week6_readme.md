## Assignment
Assignment 6: Animated Clock (Due Oct 15)

I built this clock on the concept of adding time as the fourth dimension.

When I first started thinking about how to design a clock, my mind was kind of limited to the three standard parameters we normally have in a clock: seconds, minutes, and hours. So I asked myself: what else in this world is built on exactly three core elements? And then I just realized our world model is built on the X, Y, and Z spatial axes. So, for this project, I mapped the mechanics of seconds, minutes, and hours directly onto these three spatial dimensions.

And in modern physics, time is considered the fourth dimension. Since I already had 3D space, adding time turned the piece into a functioning model of the universe. I started to imagine this clock as our Earth. Earth has its own unique time scale, so I didn’t just stop at seconds, minutes, and hours. I also visualized the real year, month, and day, allowing this "clock planet" to physically orbit and rotate in 3D space.

As for the continuously twisting, breathing tubular shapes—what i call the "worm effect"—that actually comes from a physics perspective. To physicists, the universe isn't a perfect geometric grid; it is constantly being squeezed and stretched by gravity, energy, and the flow of time itself. Spacetime is always bending and warping. I wanted to use this topological distortion to let people actually "see" how time compresses and shapes the space we live in.
